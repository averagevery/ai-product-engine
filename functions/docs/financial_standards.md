# Financial Education Standards (PISA Aligned)

## Core Objective
Lessons must build "Financial Literacy": the knowledge and understanding of financial concepts and risks, and the skills, motivation, and confidence to apply such knowledge to make effective decisions across a range of financial contexts.

## The 4 Content Pillars
Every lesson must anchor to one of these four pillars:
1. **Money and Transactions:** Everyday payments, spending, value for money, bank cards, currencies, and simple pricing (e.g., invoices, receipts, understanding VAT).
2. **Planning and Managing Finances:** Budgeting, managing income/wealth over time, understanding the impact of compounding, saving for long-term goals, and managing debt (student loans, credit cards).
3. **Risk and Reward:** Identifying financial risks (e.g., scams, phishing emails, variable interest rates, inflation) and understanding the trade-off between risk and return in investments.
4. **Financial Landscape:** Understanding the rights and responsibilities of consumers in the UK financial system, knowing where to find objective advice, and understanding basic taxation.

## Cognitive Processes (How to test them)
When writing quiz questions, the agent must test these processes:
* **Identify:** Recognise financial terms or situations (e.g., identifying a scam email).
* **Analyse:** Compare options (e.g., comparing an annual vs. monthly subscription).
* **Evaluate:** Make a judgement based on financial information.
* **Apply:** Perform a calculation or choose the best course of action (e.g., calculating the new total of an invoice if an item is removed).

## Context boundaries
Always use realistic UK contexts suitable for 16-25 year olds (e.g., first jobs, university, renting, starting to invest, avoiding modern digital scams). Use strictly UK terminology (e.g., £, ISA, PAYE, HMRC).
